# Lowering fees & getting registered — a plain-English checklist

This is the "grown-up paperwork" that quietly saves The Dollar Chain money and
makes it legitimate. None of it is urgent to *launch*, but each step below either
**cuts the fees** you pay or **unlocks tax benefits**. Do them in roughly this order.

---

## 1. Register the legal entity (do this first — everything else depends on it)

You're collecting money from the public, so you need a legal body that isn't *you
personally*. For a community fund like this, an **Incorporated Association** is the
usual, cheap, simple choice.

- **Where:** register in the state you operate from. You mentioned NSW — you *can*
  register an incorporated association in NSW and operate it, but if the activity
  and bank account are really in **QLD**, it's usually cleaner to register in QLD
  (Office of Fair Trading QLD) or register for an **ABN + consider a co-operative
  /company limited by guarantee** if you'll operate nationally. Worth a 30-minute
  chat with a not-for-profit lawyer or your accountant before you pick.
- **You'll get:** an incorporation certificate and the ability to get an **ABN**.
- **Then get an ABN:** free, online, ~15 minutes at **abr.gov.au**.

✅ **Done when:** you have an incorporation certificate + an ABN in the
association's name.

---

## 2. Apply for Stripe non-profit pricing  ← *the easy fee win*

Once you have the ABN and can show you're a not-for-profit, **ask Stripe for
discounted non-profit rates**. This changes nothing about the website — same keys,
same integration — you just pay less per transaction.

- **How:** log in to Stripe → **Support** → contact them → say:
  > "We're a registered Australian not-for-profit (ABN: ______). Do you offer
  > discounted/non-profit processing rates, and how do I apply?"
- Have your **ABN** and **incorporation certificate** ready to send.

✅ **Done when:** Stripe confirms a reduced rate on your account.

---

## 3. Register for GST  ← *claim back the GST on Stripe's fees*

Remember the 4¢ "GST" on that test charge? That's GST on **Stripe's fee**, not on
the donation. If you're **registered for GST**, you can usually **claim that back**
as an input tax credit.

- **When you must:** GST registration is compulsory once turnover hits **$150,000/yr
  for non-profits** (higher than the $75k for businesses). Below that it's optional.
- **Why register early anyway:** so you can reclaim the GST Stripe charges on its
  fees. On small volumes it's pennies; as you grow it adds up.
- **How:** through the ABN/Business Portal or your accountant. Free.

✅ **Done when:** the association is GST-registered and your bookkeeper knows to
claim input tax credits on Stripe fees.

> Note: **donations themselves stay GST-free** — they're gifts, not sales. You never
> add GST to what a member gives. This is only about reclaiming GST on your costs.

---

## 4. (Later) DGR endorsement — tax-deductible donations

**DGR = Deductible Gift Recipient.** Once endorsed, donors can claim their gift on
their own tax. This is a bigger drawcard for larger/one-off donors.

- **How:** apply via the **ACNC** (register as a charity) → then apply to the **ATO**
  for DGR endorsement. This is the most involved step and often needs an accountant.
- The FAQ already tells members: *"We're operating as a not-for-profit while we work
  through DGR registration."* Update that copy once endorsed.

✅ **Done when:** the ATO lists the association as a DGR.

---

## The fee-saving summary (what actually moves the needle)

| Lever | Effort | Saving |
|---|---|---|
| **Bundle into fewer, larger charges** (annual/quarterly) | Already built ✅ | Biggest — the 30¢ lands once, not 52× |
| **Push BECS bank debit over cards** | Already built ✅ | Large — banks cost far less than card networks |
| **Stripe non-profit rate** (step 2) | One email | Medium — lower % on every charge |
| **Reclaim GST on fees** (step 3) | Bookkeeping | Small now, grows with volume |
| Switching payment provider | High (rebuild) | ~Nil on tiny amounts — **not worth it** |

The first two are done in the product. Steps 2–4 are paperwork you do once the
association is registered, and they keep paying off forever.
